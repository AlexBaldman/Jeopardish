# JeoPARODY Brand and Scene Art Direction

The logo system is now defined in `docs/BRAND_IDENTITY_SYSTEM_V2.md`. This document
continues to own environmental art direction and scene-production rules.

## Brand Thesis

JeoPARODY is a counterfeit prestige game show broadcast from a beachside arcade cabinet.
It combines game-show authority, 16-bit immediacy, comic-book mischief, and the specific
sun-faded eccentricity of Long Beach, New York.

The visual test is simple: if the Jeopardy reference disappears, the identity must still
feel ownable. The first **O**, in **JEO**, is the interchangeable signature device. It can
become a portal, eye, donut, disco ball, coin, eclipse, or future episode-specific object
without changing the core wordmark. The added O in **PARODY** remains a stable letter.

## Logo Hierarchy

1. Read **JE + [O]** first as the familiar setup and changing visual signature.
2. Read **PARODY** as the colorful interruption and actual promise.
3. Discover that the first **O** changes while **PARODY** remains legible and stable.
4. Use **TRIVIA GOT WEIRD** as attitude, not explanation.

The logo should feel like a marquee, not a navigation label. Keep it centered in the
cabinet header and give it enough quiet space to remain the strongest fixed element.

## Interface Language

- **Gold:** commitment, score, and primary action.
- **Cyan:** progress, new clues, and navigation.
- **Pink:** reveals, surprises, and host mischief.
- **Green:** learning, voice readiness, and positive outcomes.
- **Ink navy:** shared cabinet structure; never place low-contrast navy copy on it.
- Use one bevel geometry, one hard-shadow depth, and one focus treatment across controls.
- Icon-only controls require an action-oriented hover and keyboard-focus explanation.
- Decorative detail belongs around labels, never through them.

## Scene Collection Rules

Every scene is a playable seek-and-find illustration, not generic wallpaper.

- Compose at 16:9 with a safe central gameplay zone and discoveries around the perimeter.
- Use an elevated Long Beach vantage point with the boardwalk, West End, ocean, jetties,
  rooftops, and horizon all geographically coherent.
- Build clear foreground, middle-ground, and distance planes for subtle parallax.
- Include 12-20 readable micro-stories with no repeated people, cloned props, malformed
  limbs, nonsense lettering, or objects melting into one another.
- Favor deliberate pixel clusters, inked silhouettes, readable poses, and controlled
  palettes over photorealism or over-smoothed generated texture.
- Day and night versions share composition but change events, lighting, and hidden jokes.
- Keep all humor nonessential to gameplay so the clue remains readable.

## Master Prompt

Create a production-quality 16:9 panoramic game background: an elevated wide view from a
building beside the Long Beach, New York boardwalk, looking west toward the boardwalk's
end and West End beaches stretching toward a dramatic horizon. Art direction: meticulous
late-1990s arcade pixel art enlarged with crisp nearest-neighbor detail, expressive
satirical-magazine character acting, clean graphic-novel inks, and the dense visual
storytelling of a classic seek-and-find poster. Preserve accurate human anatomy, coherent
architecture, consistent perspective, and a clearly authored palette. Leave a calmer
central upper-middle zone for a trivia panel while filling the beach, boardwalk, rooftops,
water, and frame edges with distinct readable micro-scenes. No text, logos, UI,
photorealism, painterly smearing, duplicated people, malformed hands, fused objects,
random furniture moving, or generic tropical-resort imagery.

## Scene Recipes

### West End Field Day

Late afternoon. Volleyball game, hacky-sack circle, surfers waxing boards, lifeguard rescue
drill, body surfers, kite mishap, frisbee interception, skaters guarding their friends'
backpacks, an overly serious sandcastle zoning meeting, and one tiny interchangeable-O token.

### Blue-Hour Boardwalk Broadcast

Solarized blue hour with warm boardwalk lamps, distant city glow, phosphorescent surf,
rollerbladers, amateur telescope club, suspiciously formal beach picnic, radio van, and
small supernatural broadcast interference that rewards close inspection.

### July Fourth Counterfeit Spectacular

Sunset into night, coherent fireworks reflections, crowded boardwalk, portable radios,
watermelon contest, lifeguards managing chaos, retro windbreakers, and several visual jokes
that develop across the width of the scene.

### Nor'easter Game Night

Dramatic but playful storm light, empty-beach scale, determined surfers, flapping umbrellas,
boardwalk regulars refusing to leave, a news crew losing control of its weather map, and
warm windows creating contrast without making the scene muddy.

### Sunday Morning Recovery

Bright clear morning after a huge beach day: cleanup crews, lost objects, sleepy surfers,
metal-detector rivalries, breakfast engineering, joggers, dog walkers, and quiet callbacks
to the previous night's scene.

### 1996 Time-Slip Tournament

Peak 1990s details without costume-party exaggeration: skate shoes, windbreakers, portable
CD players, disposable cameras, pagers, bodyboards, arcade flyers, and a few impossible
objects from other decades hidden as episode collectibles.

## Production Gate

Reject a scene unless it passes all five checks:

1. The location reads as Long Beach rather than a generic coast.
2. The central clue remains legible in both themes.
3. At least twelve micro-stories are independently understandable.
4. Anatomy, perspective, and object continuity survive inspection at 200 percent.
5. The scene contains memorable authored jokes without relying on generated text.
