# JeoPARODY Creative Bible v1

## Working Decision

**Title:** JeoPARODY  
**Provisional host:** Xander Trefleck  
**Front-running visual direction:** Channel O  
**Interactive comparison:** `creative-room.html`

This document supersedes **Jeopardish** as the public-facing title and treats older Malex naming as development history. Xander remains provisional until the creative-room review is complete.

## The One-Letter Thesis

`JEOPARDY` becomes `JEOPARODY` through one inserted O:

`JE + O + PAR + O + DY`

The added O between `R` and `D` is the one-letter pun that turns respectable game-show
authority into parody. The visual costume system belongs to the original O in `JEO`, so
the displayed lockup is `JE + [changing O] + PARODY`. The added O remains stable enough
to keep the word **PARODY** immediately legible.

### O-of-the-Day System

Approved starting forms:

- unauthorized broadcast portal;
- suspicious eye;
- donut;
- counterfeit coin;
- eclipse;
- disco ball.

Future forms can be tied to episodes, holidays, subject packs, story evidence, or earned
player cosmetics. The silhouette must remain circular and its position between `E` and `P`
must never move.

## Host Front-Runner: Xander Trefleck

### One-Sentence Pitch

The long-lost twin nobody knew existed, including the twin, has inherited neither the reputation nor the show but has acquired a studio key and interpreted that as destiny.

### Public Presentation

- Immaculate game-show posture.
- Precise diction and institutional calm.
- Reassuring to new players.
- Treats the show as a respected national service.
- Refers to obviously homemade objects as official broadcast equipment.

### Private Engine

- He believes history omitted him on a technicality.
- He wants recognition more than money, although he has printed a great deal of money.
- Player knowledge threatens his authority and fascinates him.
- He resents his famous twin but cannot stop defending him from outsiders.
- His villainy is mostly paperwork, vanity, and escalating attempts to control the narrative.

### Emotional Range

Xander cannot survive as a pure sneering villain. His useful contradictions are:

- vain but lonely;
- dishonest but serious about factual accuracy;
- resentful but secretly proud of excellent players;
- elegant but operating a visibly improvised network;
- desperate to control the story but constitutionally unable to stop adding details.

### Story Leak

When rattled, Xander calls the show “the inheritance.” He then claims this is a French-Canadian television term. Evidence gradually suggests he has been waiting for this opportunity much longer than his official biography allows.

## Voice Rules

The voice uses broad traits rather than imitating any one writer:

- dry Canadian understatement;
- anti-joke timing;
- formal language applied to ridiculous circumstances;
- affectionate macabre absurdity;
- concise corrections followed by unnecessary bureaucratic detail;
- the player is teased for choices, never identity;
- factual explanations remain clear even when the performance is strange.

### Rhythm

1. State the ruling plainly.
2. Pause conceptually.
3. Add one precise absurdity.
4. Leave before explaining the joke.

### Sample Lines

**Idle:** “The board is waiting. It has retained counsel.”

**Clue:** “Here is your clue. I have removed the suspicious fingerprints.”

**Correct:** “Correct. Disturbingly correct.”

**Wrong:** “No. A brave answer, in the historical sense of brave decisions.”

**Streak:** “A streak appears. I am happy for you in an administrative capacity.”

**Empty submission:** “I checked with the judges. Silence remains incorrect.”

## Visual Synthesis

### Formula

- **40% graphic novel:** silhouette, inked shadows, panel transitions, expressive close-ups.
- **35% premium pixel art:** chunky geometry, sprite-readable poses, deliberate limited detail.
- **25% editorial parody:** caricature, marginal jokes, forged seals, visual footnotes.

Pixel art is structural, not a low-resolution filter. Modern lighting, compositing, particles, and responsive motion can sit on top of sprite logic.

### Xander Design Anchors

- A recognizable game-show-host archetype without tracing a photograph.
- Silver-black hair with one suspiciously perfect wave.
- Eyebrows capable of issuing rulings independently.
- Tailored midnight jacket with an unauthorized magenta lining.
- Small maple-leaf cuff link that changes sides between scenes.
- Counterfeit trivia note, slim microphone, evidence envelope, and Channel O lapel pin.
- Sunglasses remain an occasional performance prop, not his permanent face.

### Expression Pack

Minimum production sprite/portrait states:

1. composed idle;
2. clue lean-in;
3. truth reveal;
4. reluctant approval;
5. bureaucratic disappointment;
6. empty-answer disbelief;
7. streak alarm;
8. story slip-up;
9. evidence cornered;
10. finale exposed.

## Alternate Directions Still Alive

### Counterfeit Crown / M. Alec Trebèque

The extra O becomes a forged seal. The world uses engraved banknote linework, institutional green, faded gold, and editorial caricature. His accent mark changes position when challenged.

**Strength:** richest single-image parody language.  
**Risk:** the joke may feel narrower than the larger broadcast universe.

### Substitute Signal / Gord Trebeckett

The extra O becomes a station-identification test pattern. The world uses CRT bloom, wood paneling, local-station graphics, and handmade props. Gord arrived to cover one episode in 1987 and has interpreted silence as renewal.

**Strength:** warm, lovable, immediately legible.  
**Risk:** weaker mystery and less premium visual authority.

### Alias Option / Sander LeFaux

An elegant, less derivative public alias whose credentials were printed at a pharmacy.

**Strength:** strongest standalone IP distance.  
**Risk:** loses some immediate game-show-reference recognition.

## Logo Production Rules

- Always spell the title `JeoPARODY` in prose when mixed case is useful.
- Visually emphasize `PAR` and the inserted `O`, without breaking whole-word readability.
- Keep the O circular, animated sparingly, and clickable where interaction is appropriate.
- Use a static O inside navigation links to avoid nested interactive controls.
- Preserve a one-color version for small icons, print, and accessibility modes.
- Do not use the old Jeopardish title image in new public surfaces.
